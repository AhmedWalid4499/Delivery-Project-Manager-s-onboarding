🌐 Orange Business — DPM Process Guide

WAN Delivery Process

The end-to-end Delivery Project Manager process for a WAN / SD-WAN site — from local validation and carrier circuit delivery through router installation, cutover, and hand-over. Built from the WAN Project Timeline.

🌐 What is a WAN delivery?

A WAN delivery connects a customer site to the wide-area network — typically migrating it onto a new SD-WAN solution. Unlike a LAN migration (where the hardware is already inside the building), a WAN delivery depends on a third-party carrier physically delivering a circuit to the site first. That makes it a longer, more coordination-heavy process: the carrier, the customer's local team, and the Orange Business (OB) delivery team all have to act in the right order.

As the DPM, most of your job is chasing dependencies you don't directly control — the carrier's survey and installation dates, and the customer completing their in-house wiring — then landing a clean cutover once the OB routers are in.

🤝 Why so many hand-offs?

Three parties own different pieces. OB validates, orders, and installs the routers. The carrier surveys and installs the physical circuit and NTU. The customer's local team grants site access, gets landlord approvals, completes the in-house wiring to the server room, and runs acceptance tests. If any one stalls, the whole delivery slips.

📞 The biggest dependency

The carrier and the customer's local team. The order can't even be placed until the customer validates the scope, site details, and circuit DMARC points. Survey and install dates hinge on the customer granting access — and sometimes on landlord approvals. Keep both moving.

✅ What does success look like?

The circuit is installed and handed to OB, the OB routers are racked and connected, the LAN is activated during the agreed change window, the customer's testers confirm the service works, and — after the hyper-care period — the site is handed over to operations.

🗓️ The four stages

Validate & Order (phases 1–3) → Circuit Delivery (phases 4–8, carrier-led) → Router Install & Migration Prep (phases 9–12) → Migration & HOTO (phases 13–14). Carrier lead times (survey → install → handover) dominate the timeline; the cutover itself is a single change window.

Key terms: DMARC = the demarcation point where the carrier's circuit ends and the customer's wiring begins · NTU = Network Termination Unit, the carrier device at the site edge · SAT = Service Acceptance Test · CAB = Change Advisory Board (approves the cutover) · hyper care = the close-support period right after go-live before hand-over to operations.
