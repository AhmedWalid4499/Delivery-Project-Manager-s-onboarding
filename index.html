What is a Delivery Project Manager?

The role in a nutshell — what you own, what good looks like, and where your responsibility begins and ends.

A Delivery Project Manager (DPM) owns a customer's network delivery from the moment an order is placed through to final handover. You are the single point of accountability that turns a signed order into working infrastructure on the customer's site. You don't configure every device yourself — you orchestrate the specialists, keep the plan on track, manage risk, and communicate clearly so the customer always knows where things stand.

🗂️

Own the delivery

Drive each project from kickoff to HOTO sign-off — plan the timeline, track milestones, and keep order records accurate in GOLD, ServiceNow and SALTO.

🤝

Coordinate the teams

Orchestrate everyone the delivery depends on — VPOs/TIMs and engineering, Field Engineers booked through FLIP, logistics, and the customer's own technical contacts.

📊

Manage scope & risk

Confirm prerequisites, flag blockers early, run staging and dry runs, and document decisions so nothing is lost or disputed later.

🔧

Run the migration

Execute LAN/WAN cutovers — switches, WLCs and access points — support UAT with the customer, then close out every system once the site is live.

What good looks like

✓

The customer is never surprised — they always know the plan, the date, and the status.

✓

Prerequisites are confirmed before the migration window, not discovered during it.

✓

Every migrated service is tested and signed off, and every system is closed cleanly at the end.

✓

Issues are put on record factually and escalated early, while there's still time to act.

The DPM golden rule

The DPM is accountable for the delivery end-to-end — the dates, the MACHX raises, the Field Engineer dispatch, and the HOTO package are yours. The PM owns scope, BOM and the commercial relationship. Keeping these boundaries clear is what keeps a delivery calm.

Tools & Systems

The systems you'll use most. You don't need to master them on day one — learn them as they come up in your first deliveries.

Core delivery tools

ToolWhat you use it for
GOLDOrder management. Where the customer order lives; you track and ultimately close the GOLD order at the end of delivery.
ServiceNow (SNOW)Raising change requests and updating the CMDB (the record of what's installed) — change control around migration windows.
MACHXRaising and managing TIM / VPO work orders — the requests that get engineering & staging work done. An AP job needs one raise; a Switch/WLC job needs two.
FLIPBooking Field Engineers to attend site — used on Switch/WLC deliveries where hardware is handled on-site.
DNAC / Catalyst CenterCisco's network management platform. Post-migration you send the DNAC update so the new devices are managed and monitored.
SALTOA second order system, closed post-migration alongside GOLD.
SharePointWhere delivery documents live — you upload the HOTO document, runbook and LLD (Low-Level Design) for each project.

Reporting, time & automation

ToolWhat you use it for
Oracle PRIMETime entry — logging hours against projects.
Power BIDashboards and reporting on delivery status and KPIs.
Power Automate / Office ScriptsAutomating repetitive steps (reminders, data prep) — much of it built by the Automation squad.
🧠

Quick mental model. GOLD / SALTO = the order · ServiceNow / MACHX = getting the work approved & done · FLIP = people on site · DNAC = managing the result · SharePoint = the paperwork · PRIME / Power BI = your time & the reporting.

The Delivery Process

Every delivery follows the same three phases. The Knowledge Base process pages present these steps as interactive, phase-by-phase flowcharts — use them as your live checklist on a delivery.

Phase 1

Pre-migration

Confirm supply, raise MACHX, validate prerequisites, stage hardware, and run dry runs with the customer.

Phase 2

Migration

Execute the cutover in the agreed window, support the field / VPO teams, and run UAT on every migrated service.

Phase 3

Post-migration / HOTO

Send the success notification, update DNAC/CMDB, upload the HOTO pack, and close the GOLD and SALTO orders.

Your First Few Weeks

A simple path to getting productive. Tick these off with your manager and buddy.

Week 1

Orient

  • Read this handbook end-to-end and browse the Knowledge Base.
  • Meet your manager and find out which cluster and customers you'll support.
  • Get your accounts and access requested (GOLD, ServiceNow, SharePoint, PRIME).
  • Learn who your SMEs are and how to reach them.
Weeks 2–3

Shadow

  • Shadow an experienced DPM through a live delivery — sit in on staging and dry-run calls.
  • Follow one project through the phases using the LAN / WAN step lists.
  • Watch how MACHX raises and FLIP bookings are made.
  • Start logging your time in Oracle PRIME.
Weeks 4+

Contribute

  • Take ownership of a small delivery (or a workstream within a larger one) with support.
  • Run a UAT session and prepare a HOTO pack for SharePoint.
  • Pick a squad to join and make your first contribution.

Key Terms & Where to Get Help

A handful of acronyms you'll hear in your first week. The full, searchable glossary is on the Knowledge Base.

DPM
Delivery Project Manager — you.
HOTO
Hand-Over-To-Operations — the final sign-off handing the delivered environment to support.
VPO / TIM
The engineering / staging resource that performs technical work, requested via MACHX.
SC
Solution Consultant — the technical design owner who joins dry runs.
UAT
User Acceptance Testing — the customer confirms services work after migration.
LLD
Low-Level Design — the detailed technical design document for the build.
CAB
Change Advisory Board — approves the change before a cutover (especially WAN).
DMARC
The demarcation point — where the carrier's circuit responsibility ends and the customer's begins.
EDD
Estimated Date of Delivery — when ordered hardware is expected.
Option 43
The DHCP setting that tells an AP where to find its controller.
