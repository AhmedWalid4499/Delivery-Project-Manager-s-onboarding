WLC & Switch Migration Process

Orange Business — Delivery Project Manager end-to-end process guide. Built from the MLAN (WLC & Switches) project plan.

🔌 What is a Switch / WLC Migration?

A Switch or WLC migration replaces old network switches or Wireless LAN Controllers at a customer site with new Cisco Catalyst hardware. Unlike APs, switches are the backbone of the network — every device in the building plugs into a switch. A failed migration can take a whole floor or building offline, which is why this flavour has more steps, a formal staging session, a Field Engineer on site, an external dry run, and two MACHX raises.

🤔 Why is it more complex than APs?

Switches carry all traffic — phones, PCs, cameras, printers, APs, servers. Replacing one affects everything connected to it. That's why there's an external dry run with the customer, a staging session to pre-configure the hardware, a Field Engineer physically present for the cutover, and IP addresses pulled from the TDT up front.

✅ What does success look like?

All migrated switches are live and carrying traffic. The customer confirms all phones, PCs, and services work. CMDB and DNAC are updated, the GOLD and SALTO orders are closed, and the HOTO document with runbook and LLD is uploaded to SharePoint and signed off.

⚠️ The most common failure points

1. 3rd-party servers not checked during the external dry run — servers from vendors like DXC may have special VLAN or routing needs not in the standard runbook. 2. Staging skipped or rushed — if the new switch isn't pre-configured to match the old one exactly, the cutover will fail.

🗓️ The four phases

Ordering (GOLD/SALTO orders, EDD, warehouse) → Pre-Migration (IPs from TDT, external dry run, Field Engineer, staging, two MACHX raises) → During Migration (cutover & testing) → Post-Migration (partial/success email, CMDB/DNAC, close orders, HOTO). Migrations usually run in maintenance windows; multi-day cutovers need a partial-migration email each day.

Who's who: DPM = you (Delivery Project Manager) · VPO = the operations engineer who stages and migrates the devices · SC = Solution Consultant (runbook + LLD) · Field Engineer = on-site hardware, booked via FLIP · TDT = the team that provides the IP addressing · ODM = Order Delivery Manager · HOTO = Hand-Over-To-Operations.
